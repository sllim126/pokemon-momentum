I've written two posts now about what master sets *cost*: [the first one](/articles/lets-talk-about-master-sets) priced every Scarlet & Violet and Mega Evolution set, and [Part 2](/articles/master-sets-part-2-japanese-vs-english) compared English and Japanese sets and asked whether you even need the bulk.

But the numbers are only half the story. Something has changed about *how* people collect master sets over the last few months, and it's one of the best things to happen to the hobby.

## Master sets have turned into a craft

When you can't find product on the shelf, you don't stop caring about your collection. You put your time and attention somewhere else. A lot of collectors have put it into making their binders absolutely *beautiful*.

- **The "Michi method."** Instead of nine, twelve or sixteen cards on every page, collectors leave gaps and fill them with printed art, Pokémon images, set logos and little notes, so each page reads like a display rather than storage. Search for it and you'll lose an afternoon.
- **Custom binders.** Etsy and independent artists are selling custom binder artwork, zipper pull key chains, spine labels and page inserts made for specific sets or specific Pokémon. The binder itself has become just as much part of the collection as the cards are. For ideas, [Card Binder Ideas](https://www.cardbinderideas.com/covers) has a gallery of hand-painted binder covers made by collectors.
- **Placeholders and title pages.** Printed placeholders for the cards you're still chasing, and custom divider pages between the base set, the reverse holos and the secret rares. Our [Placeholder Library](/placeholders) has free printable ones for every Scarlet & Violet and Mega Evolution set.
- **Full-page art.** One picture cut into card-size pieces so a whole binder page becomes a single image. We built a free [Binder Art Maker](/binder-art) for exactly this: pick a picture, choose 2×2 up to 4×4, add a set logo if you want, and print it with cut marks.

*Hand-painted Jirachi binder cover by Saku ([@saku.vault](https://www.instagram.com/p/Db0q6p0G4Jx/)), shown with their permission.*

Honestly? It's starting to look like a gateway into scrapbooking. And I mean that as a compliment. Pokémon Japan even leaned into it. Their official video for Masayoshi Oishi's ["Pokémon All Stars 1025"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PqOBtBOsB_I) is a beautiful piece built around an actual scrapbook-style Pokédex. Turns out "gotta catch 'em all" and "gotta paste 'em all" scratch that same itch.

The Pokémon Company noticed too. On November 6 it posted [official printable binder inserts for Delta Reign](https://www.pokemon.com/us/features/collect-pokemon-tcg-mega-evolution-delta-reign-with-printable-binder-inserts): a [placeholder for every card in the set](https://www.pokemon.com/static-assets/cms3/us/pdf/trading-card-game/2026/binder-inserts/me06-placeholder-inserts-en.pdf), with its name and number, and a set of [art inserts](https://www.pokemon.com/static-assets/cms3/us/pdf/trading-card-game/2026/binder-inserts/me06-art-insert-en.pdf) for building your own mini collections. Print, cut, slide them in. When the company that makes the cards starts publishing binder art, it's not a fad anymore.

![A Delta Reign binder open on two pages, with cards mixed in among full-size art inserts. Image: The Pokémon Company.](/media/1200/delta-reign-official-binder-inserts-038b5ec031.webp)

What I love about it is that it's the opposite of the stress I wrote about [the first time](/articles/lets-talk-about-master-sets). Arranging a binder page doesn't depend on what's in stock or what a card sold for yesterday. It's just you, your cards and a free evening (evenings, if we're being honest).

## Not every master set is a set

When people say "master set," they usually mean one expansion: every card, every reverse holo, every promo. But that's just one way to do it. Some other kinds of master sets I'm seeing people building:

- **One Pokémon.** Every Eevee, every Gengar, every Lillie's Clefairy ever printed. You'll never run out of things to chase, and you pick your own pace.
- **One artist.** Pick an illustrator whose style you love and collect their work across sets. These binders end up looking like art books. Yuki Morii, Shinji Kanda and others are getting their time and attention from the fans, and it's great hearing other collectors talk about the artwork and not just the price.
- **One type.** All the Fire types from every era, or every Psychic illustration rare, or every Amazing Rare card. Whatever the collection, they look great when you lay the pages out together.
- **One era or one language.** Every secret rare from Scarlet & Violet, or a Japanese-only binder. ([Japanese can be a lot cheaper](/articles/master-sets-part-2-japanese-vs-english), depending on the set.)
- **Promos only.** Every promo from an era. These sets are smaller and some are cheaper, but some are genuinely hard to find and can be quite expensive to obtain.
- **Whatever you want.** A Pokédex in order ([like this one](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PMLhznCTsRU), an illustration card for every Pokémon), one card for every Pokémon you've ever had on a team, every card with a beach in the background, or an apple, or that look like they're pooping. If it makes you happy to flip through, it counts.

There's no master set police. You make the rules, and you decide when it's done.

## So where do you start?

- **Can't find packs?** Pick a Pokémon, an artist or a type and start a binder you can build one single at a time.
- **Already have a binder?** Pick your favorite page and redo just that one, Michi style. See if you like it before you redo the whole thing. The [Binder Art Maker](/binder-art) makes a good first page.
- **Want it to feel finished?** Print [placeholders](/placeholders) for what's missing. A binder with no empty pockets feels done long before it actually is.
- **Add title cards.** I put title cards in my [Journey Together master set](/articles/journey-together-master-set-checklist-and-promo-guide) to remember which product each card came from and to break the binder up a bit: all the promos, then the prize pack cards, then the Japanese promos, and so on. You can [download them](/placeholders) from the Placeholder Library too.

Collect what makes you happy, set a budget before you start, and then go make it pretty. The best master set is the one you enjoy finishing, and enjoy looking at.
