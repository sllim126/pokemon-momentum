I've written two posts now about what master sets *cost*: [the first one](/articles/lets-talk-about-master-sets) priced every Scarlet & Violet and Mega Evolution set, and [Part 2](/articles/master-sets-part-2-japanese-vs-english) compared English and Japanese sets and asked whether you even need the bulk.

But the numbers are only half the story. Something has changed about *how* people collect master sets over the last few months, and I think it's one of the best things to happen to the hobby in a while.

## Master sets have turned into a craft

When you can't find product on the shelf, you don't stop caring about your collection. You put your time and attention somewhere else. A lot of collectors have put it into making their binders *beautiful*.

- **The "Michi method."** Instead of nine cards on every page, collectors leave gaps and fill them with printed art, Pokémon images, set logos and little notes, so each page reads like a display rather than storage. Search for it and you'll lose an afternoon.
- **Custom binders.** Etsy and independent artists are selling binder covers, spine labels and page inserts made for specific sets or specific Pokémon. The binder itself has become part of the collection.
- **Placeholders and title pages.** Printed placeholders for the cards you're still chasing, and custom divider pages between the base set, the reverse holos and the secret rares. Our [Placeholder Library](/placeholders) has free printable ones for every Scarlet & Violet and Mega Evolution set.
- **Full-page art.** One picture cut into card-size pieces so a whole binder page becomes a single image. We built a free [Binder Art Maker](/binder-art) for exactly this: pick a picture, choose 2×2 up to 4×4, add a set logo if you want, and print it with cut marks.

Honestly? It's starting to look like a gateway into scrapbooking. And I mean that as a compliment. Pokémon Japan even leaned into it. Their official video for Masayoshi Oishi's ["Pokémon All Stars 1025"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PqOBtBOsB_I) is a beautiful piece built around an actual scrapbook-style Pokédex. Turns out "gotta catch 'em all" and "gotta paste 'em all" scratch the same itch.

The Pokémon Company noticed too. On November 6 it posted [official printable binder inserts for Delta Reign](https://www.pokemon.com/us/features/collect-pokemon-tcg-mega-evolution-delta-reign-with-printable-binder-inserts): a [placeholder for every card in the set](https://www.pokemon.com/static-assets/cms3/us/pdf/trading-card-game/2026/binder-inserts/me06-placeholder-inserts-en.pdf), with its name and number, and a set of [art inserts](https://www.pokemon.com/static-assets/cms3/us/pdf/trading-card-game/2026/binder-inserts/me06-art-insert-en.pdf) for building your own mini collections. Print, cut, slide them in. When the company that makes the cards starts publishing binder art, it's not a fad anymore.

![A Delta Reign binder open on two pages, with cards mixed in among full-size art inserts. Image: The Pokémon Company.](/media/1200/delta-reign-official-binder-inserts-038b5ec031.webp)

What I love about it is that it's the opposite of the stress I wrote about [the first time](/articles/lets-talk-about-master-sets). Arranging a binder page doesn't depend on what's in stock or what a card sold for yesterday. It's just you, your cards and a free evening.

## Not every master set is a set

When people say "master set," they usually mean one expansion: every card, every reverse holo, every promo. But that's just one way to do it. Some other kinds of master sets I see people building:

- **One Pokémon.** Every Eevee, every Gengar, every Lillie's Clefairy ever printed. You'll never run out of things to chase, and you pick your own pace.
- **One artist.** Pick an illustrator whose style you love and collect their work across sets. These binders end up looking like art books.
- **One type.** All the Fire types from an era, or every Psychic illustration rare. It looks great when you lay the pages out together.
- **One era or one language.** Every secret rare from Scarlet & Violet, or a Japanese-only binder. ([Japanese can be a lot cheaper](/articles/master-sets-part-2-japanese-vs-english), depending on the set.)
- **Promos only.** Every promo from an era. These are smaller and usually cheaper, but some are genuinely hard to find.
- **Whatever you want.** A Pokédex in order, one card for every Pokémon you've ever had on a team, every card with a beach in the background. If it makes you happy to flip through, it counts.

There's no master set police. You make the rules, and you decide when it's done.

## So where do you start?

- **Can't find packs?** Pick a Pokémon, an artist or a type and start a binder you can build one single at a time.
- **Already have a binder?** Pick your favorite page and redo just that one, Michi style. See if you like it before you redo the whole thing. The [Binder Art Maker](/binder-art) makes a good first page.
- **Want it to feel finished?** Print [placeholders](/placeholders) for what's missing. A binder with no empty pockets feels done long before it actually is.

Collect what makes you happy, set a budget before you start, and then go make it pretty. The best master set is the one you enjoy finishing, and enjoy looking at.
