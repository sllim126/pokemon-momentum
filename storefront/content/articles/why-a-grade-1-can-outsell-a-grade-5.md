You'd expect graded prices to climb with the grade: a 1 is the worst condition a grader will put in a slab, so it should be the cheapest. For most Pokémon cards it isn't. A Grade 1 usually sells for more than a Grade 5, and often for more than the same card ungraded.

*Graded prices are from [PriceCharting](https://www.pricecharting.com/category/pokemon-cards) as of November 23, 2026. Their grades 1 to 9 cover any grading company, not only PSA. I compared every Pokémon card that has both a Grade 1 and a Grade 5 price: 4,677 cards, English and Japanese.*

## How often a 1 beats a 5

- **83%** of those cards had a Grade 1 price above their Grade 5 price.
- The typical Grade 1 sold for about **2.4 times** the Grade 5.
- **78%** sold above the ungraded card, and 13% even above a Grade 9.

It isn't a one-day quirk either. On October 9, the first day I have this data, it was the same 83%.

## Cheap cards have the biggest premium

The premium depends on what the card is worth. Grouped by the Grade 5 price:

- **Under $20:** the Grade 1 was higher 97% of the time, typically 5.5 times as much
- **$20 to $100:** higher 93% of the time, about 2.4 times
- **$100 to $500:** about a coin flip (53%)
- **Over $500:** higher only 20% of the time; here the normal order mostly holds

So a beat-up copy of a $10 card can be worth several times a mid-grade one, while a beat-up Base Set Charizard is still worth less than a better one.

## Some examples

Ungraded → Grade 1 → Grade 5 → Grade 9, from PriceCharting:

- **[Rayquaza VMAX #218, Evolving Skies](https://www.pricecharting.com/game/pokemon-evolving-skies/rayquaza-vmax-218):** $820 → **$1,035** → $808 → $951
- **[Mega Charizard X ex #125, Phantasmal Flames](https://www.pricecharting.com/game/pokemon-phantasmal-flames/mega-charizard-x-ex-125):** $523 → **$728** → $242 → $510
- **[Pikachu with Grey Felt Hat #85](https://www.pricecharting.com/game/pokemon-promo/pikachu-with-grey-felt-hat-85):** $785 → **$1,890** → $660 → $786
- **[Umbreon ex #161, Prismatic Evolutions](https://www.pricecharting.com/game/pokemon-prismatic-evolutions/umbreon-ex-161):** $1,075 → **$800** → $464 → $977

And the exception, an expensive vintage card where grade still wins:

- **[Charizard #4, Base Set](https://www.pricecharting.com/game/pokemon-base-set/charizard-4):** $342 → $361 → $411 → $2,426

## Why it happens

Two things, as far as I can tell:

1. **Hardly anyone grades a wrecked card.** Grading costs more than most damaged cards are worth, so very few Grade 1s exist. Mid grades are what you get when someone hoped for a 9 or 10 and missed, and there are plenty of those.
2. **Some collectors want the 1s.** The worst possible copy is a collection of its own, and a few people chase complete sets of Grade 1s. With so few copies around, a couple of buyers are enough to set the price.

The second point is also the catch: a Grade 1 price often rests on only a handful of sales. One collector paying up can move it a long way.

## What to do with this

- **Got a badly damaged copy of a popular card?** Don't write it off. A Grade 1 slab of a chase card can be worth more than you'd expect, sometimes more than a clean ungraded copy.
- **Pricing one?** Look at the actual recent sales, not just the headline number. With so few sales, the last one might have been a single collector filling a gap.
- **Expensive vintage is different.** Over $500, the higher grade is still worth more most of the time.
